About

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in the Department of Economics at Rutgers University, New Brunswick. I hold a B.Econ. in Economics of Resources and Environment from Renmin University of China and spent a term as an exchange student at UC Davis.

My research asks how climate and land-use change reshape energy systems and agricultural outcomes, and what that means for policy. I rely on applied econometrics, spatial data, and causal inference.

Research

Integrating Climate Change Impacts into the Assessment of China’s Onshore Wind Power Potential

With Dr. Yanhua Wang. Under review at Environmental Impact Assessment Review.

Abstract

China is highly exposed to climate change impacts, while onshore wind power is expected to play a central role in achieving its 2060 carbon neutrality target. Assessing the impacts of climate change on wind energy potential is critical for sustainable energy planning. This study develops an integrated framework to evaluate China’s onshore wind potential by incorporating wind speed, temperature, and precipitation into geographical, technical, and economic assessments under GCM–SSP projections. High-resolution, multi-model results reveal that approximately one-third of China’s land area remains suitable for onshore wind development, with national geographic variations generally within ±6% across scenarios. Annual technical potential (10,500–14,200 TWh) exhibits modest fluctuations rather than persistent decline, with mid-century changes typically within 2% of current levels, indicating overall resilience of the aggregate resource base. However, regional disparities intensify: northwestern provinces retain dominant generation capacity and lower climate-related economic penalties, whereas humid southern regions face rising precipitation-induced turbine degradation. Levelized cost of electricity increases by 9%, 11%, and nearly 20% under SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5, respectively, potentially offsetting localized technical gains. These findings suggest that while China’s aggregate wind resource remains structurally stable under climate change, regional economic competitiveness and climate uncertainties necessitate differentiated deployment strategies and climate-informed energy planning.

Sand Out, Water In: The (Un)intended Consequences of Afforestation on the Hydrological Cycle and Agricultural Outcomes

Work in progress, based on my undergraduate thesis.

Abstract

Large-scale afforestation in arid regions is widely debated, yet its spatial externalities, particularly those transmitted through atmospheric processes, remain poorly understood. This paper studies the spatial externalities of afforestation on agricultural productivity through atmospheric moisture spillovers. Using quasi-random variation in prevailing wind direction, we construct upwind forest exposure measures that capture the upstream air masses reaching each location in China. We establish a positive relationship between forest exposure and annual precipitation. This effect, however, does not extend to the growing season, consistent with a redistribution of atmospheric moisture rather than a uniform increase in rainfall. Nevertheless, crop yields increase significantly in downwind areas, even conditional on local precipitation and weather controls. Crop phenology shows that yield gains are concentrated in growth stages most sensitive to moisture stress, indicating non-precipitation moisture channels. By identifying mechanism-specific atmospheric elasticities, the paper highlights a spatial decoupling between forest location and agricultural benefits and underscores the importance of accounting for cross-regional externalities in the evaluation of land-use and climate policies.

Agent-Based Policy Simulation of Express Packaging Waste Management

Project leader, Renmin University of China, 2024–2025. Awarded “Excellent Completion.”

Survey data from 1,600 consumers, merchants, and couriers parameterize an agent-based model of “Reduce, Replace, Recycle” policies. Non-monetary incentives outperform traditional subsidies in encouraging merchants to cut packaging.
